# Jeeves: plan to win the first paying customers

Prepared 19 September 2026. Planning document only; no product, pricing, website, deployment, or customer-data policy changes are authorized by this document.

**Recommendation:** start selling a fixed-scope, human-led AI governance evidence pilot to healthcare AI vendors with an active buyer review. Use Jeeves to demonstrate the workflow. Build customer-operated software against a committed design partner and explicit readiness gates.

The immediate objective is a paid engagement that proves a specific problem is worth solving. The longer-term objective is repeatable software revenue. Track these separately: consulting fees are not SaaS ARR, and vendor procurement work does not validate demand for a payer's internal governance platform.

Assumptions: founder-led selling; limited engineering capacity; US healthcare relationships are the starting hypothesis; no existing customer pipeline or budget was supplied. Prices, activity counts, conversion targets, and schedules below are hypotheses, not market benchmarks or forecasts. If warm payer relationships already exist, use the payer alternative in section 1.

## 1. Choose one buyer and one problem for the first campaign

The current marketing has two audiences. The main product demonstrates healthcare organizations governing their AI portfolio; `/pilot` offers procurement-readiness services to vendors selling into healthcare. These require different buyers, evidence, and sales conversations.

| Route | First customer and buying trigger | What they buy | Validation obtained |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recommended first-revenue test** | Healthcare AI vendor with a live health-plan or health-system procurement review, a questionnaire deadline within 90 days, and missing governance evidence | A four-week evidence-readiness engagement for one product and one buyer review | Willingness to pay for evidence preparation and maintenance |
| **Direct platform route** | Regional payer or healthcare organization with a warm executive sponsor, recurring AI requests, and a demonstrable review backlog | A paid design partnership for one internal intake-to-decision workflow; operational use follows readiness gates | Willingness to pay for governance workflow software |

Both independent reviewers favor the vendor route for the initial revenue test. This is a strategic recommendation, not evidence that vendors will buy. If the founder's goal is specifically payer-side SaaS, choose the second route and accept that identity, security review, and procurement are part of the first sale. Do not count one segment's success as proof of the other.

For the recommended route:

- **Economic buyer:** CEO/COO at a small vendor, or a revenue/product executive who owns the delayed commercial outcome and budget.
- **Working champion:** security/compliance lead, AI governance lead, or product operations owner responsible for assembling evidence.
- **User group:** one product owner, one evidence owner, and the necessary domain reviewers.
- **Qualification:** an actual review, a dated business consequence, a named budget owner, permission to work with appropriate source material, and willingness to pay.
- **Disqualify or defer:** exploratory AI interest without a buying event; free-demo-only requests; requirements for PHI, production integrations, certification, guaranteed procurement approval, or an unrestricted bespoke platform.

Proposed pitch: **“Jeeves helps healthcare AI teams organize buyer-requested governance evidence, identify missing proof, and assign accountable owners through a structured review workflow.”**

The healthcare category is competitive. Credo AI already markets healthcare inventory, assessments, approval gates, and evidence; OneTrust markets intake, risk scoring, approvals, and controls; ServiceNow markets enterprise AI governance connected to its workflow platform. These are vendor-described capabilities, not independently tested comparisons. The proposed Jeeves advantage to test is a narrow engagement, clear deliverables, and hands-on setup for an urgent review. Healthcare branding and AI-written drafts alone are not established differentiation. Sources: [Credo AI healthcare](https://www.credo.ai/industry/healthcare), [OneTrust AI Governance](https://www.onetrust.com/solutions/ai-governance/), [ServiceNow AI Control Tower](https://www.servicenow.com/products/ai-control-tower.html), reviewed 19 September 2026.

## 2. Sell this initial offer

**Offer name:** Jeeves Governance Evidence Pilot.

**Scope:** four weeks, one AI product, one active buyer questionnaire, up to 50 distinct agreed requirements, two one-hour customer review sessions, and four 30-minute weekly check-ins. All meetings are included in the delivery effort. Confirm the requirement count and source availability during scoping; larger engagements need a separate scope.

| Deliverable | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| Requirement-to-evidence register | Each agreed requirement links to a source/version, named owner, and status: supported, partial, missing, or not applicable with rationale |
| Human-reviewed response pack | Responses distinguish verified evidence, customer attestations, and unresolved gaps; the customer's accountable owner approves external use |
| Gap and remediation plan | Every material gap has an owner, next action, and target date; unverified claims remain visibly unverified |
| Jeeves workflow demonstration | Fictional documents show submission, reviewer return, revision, acceptance, and the decision record; all simulated behavior is labeled |
| Handoff and maintenance recommendation | Customer receives usable files in an agreed format and knows how to update them when product/evidence changes |

The first engagement is delivered with manual analysis and customer-approved document tools. Automated questionnaire ingestion, response-pack export, and source-document AI analysis are not established current Jeeves capabilities. Do not make delivery depend on implementing them.

**Data boundary:** keep real customer material out of the current Jeeves demo, including redacted company documents. Use the customer's approved workspace and agreed access/retention terms for the service. Put only fictional substitutes into Jeeves. No PHI is in scope. Do not send customer material to an LLM without explicit customer authorization and an agreed provider/data arrangement.

Customer commitments: appoint an evidence owner and executive sponsor, provide the agreed material in week 1, and review drafts within three business days. Missing inputs change the schedule or leave explicit gaps; they never justify invented evidence.

Sequence: week 1 scope and baseline; week 2 evidence mapping; week 3 owner review and corrections; week 4 final pack and handoff. If specialist legal, clinical, privacy, or security judgment is required, involve a qualified reviewer and price that effort. Jeeves does not confer that expertise.

## 3. Test pricing with real proposals

| Package | Initial price hypothesis | Limits |
|---|---:|---|
| Optional scoping sprint | **$2,500** | One week, up to eight delivery hours; process map, evidence inventory, fixed pilot proposal; credit once against a pilot signed within 30 days |
| Governance Evidence Pilot | **$12,000 fixed** | Four-week scope above; proposed 50% at kickoff and 50% at handoff, subject to agreed contract terms |
| Managed evidence maintenance | **$1,500–$3,000/month** | Quote only after observing recurring work; define product count, refresh cadence, and included hours; this is a service fee |
| Future software design-partner plan | **$24,000–$36,000/year** | A separate willingness-to-pay test after customer-use gates; define users, initiatives, support, storage, and AI usage before quoting |

Discovery is optional, not an extra hurdle for a qualified buyer ready to sign the pilot. Do not double-charge credited work. When a scoping sprint converts, its hours count within the total 50-hour planning envelope below; they are not eight additional hours. Do not promise recurring software access as part of the current service engagement.

Check the economics before quoting. Illustrative planning calculation: 50 delivery hours at an internal cost of $100/hour plus $1,500 of specialist/tools cost gives $6,500 direct cost. At $12,000, contribution before sales time and general overhead is $5,500, about 46%. These are assumed costs, not a spending authorization. If the work repeatedly exceeds that envelope, narrow scope or reprice.

Offer the same clear scope to the first three qualified prospects and record actual purchase decisions. Capture whether losses reflect urgency, trust, budget owner, scope, or price. Avoid discounting before understanding the objection; exchange any founder concession for narrower scope or structured feedback, never a required positive testimonial.

For software, establish recurring value separately. At eight reviews/month, three hours saved/review, and a customer-agreed $100/hour value, annual labor savings would be $28,800. That alone does not justify a $30,000 annual subscription. Measure actual volume and time, then decide whether the economics work. Do not invent avoided fines or treat a delayed deal's full value as attributable ROI.

## 4. Run a measurable 90-day sales campaign

| Period | Founder-owned actions | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–7 | Choose the segment; list 30 target accounts; request warm introductions; prepare the one-page offer, fictional sample pack, and eight-minute demo; scope marketing corrections | One coherent offer, working contact path, and no unsupported customer-use promises |
| Days 8–21 | Seek 12–15 discovery conversations; ask to see the shape of a real questionnaire in the buyer's approved environment; record deadline, owner, existing process, alternatives, and budget path | At least five describe the same urgent problem; at least three agree to scoped proposal discussions |
| Days 22–30 | Send three fixed-scope proposals; involve the economic buyer; agree inputs, acceptance, data handling, and payment | One signed, paid pilot is the target; no payment after three qualified proposals triggers a loss review and one focused revision |
| Days 31–60 | Deliver the first engagement; measure effort and rework; hold weekly customer check-ins; continue prospecting | Customer accepts deliverables and confirms whether the pack was used; delivery economics are documented |
| Days 61–90 | Sell a second pilot or paid continuation; request permission for a case study; decide which repeated task merits software investment | Two paid engagements or one paid engagement plus a paid continuation; separate decision on a funded software design partner |

These are operating targets. Enterprise procurement or product readiness may extend the schedule. A software subscription is conditional on readiness and customer acceptance, not promised by day 90.

Weekly founder cadence: five tailored first contacts, three follow-ups, and two discovery calls when the pipeline supports them. Begin with former colleagues, healthcare founders, security leaders, and specialist advisers who can make relevant introductions. Test channel partners after direct conversations establish a repeatable problem. Delay paid advertising and broad content production until the offer converts.

Use a simple pipeline with: account, segment, champion, economic buyer, trigger/deadline, problem evidence, approved next step/date, stage, proposed value, loss reason, and delivery obligations. Stages: contacted → discovery → qualified → proposal → paid → delivered → renewed. Interest and demo attendance are not revenue.

Discovery questions:

1. Which specific AI review or buyer request is stalled, and what happens if it remains unresolved?
2. Who owns the response, who approves it, and who can authorize spending?
3. What source evidence already exists, and what is actually missing?
4. How long did the last comparable review take, including evidence gathering and rework?
5. What do existing spreadsheets, GRC tools, advisers, or internal teams fail to solve?
6. What would make a four-week, $12,000 engagement worth buying, and what must procurement approve first?

Draft outreach, for the founder to adapt and send:

> I'm developing Jeeves for healthcare AI teams assembling governance evidence for buyer reviews. We're testing a four-week engagement around one product and one questionnaire: source-linked answers, explicit evidence gaps, and named owners. Are you dealing with an active health-plan or health-system review where that work is slowing the response? If so, I'd like to understand the bottleneck and see whether the scope fits.

Demo sequence: buyer problem (1 minute), requirement/evidence checklist (2), reviewer return and revised submission (2), human decision/audit record (2), exact pilot deliverables and next step (1). Explain that identities and data in the demo are fictional. Lead with the evidence workflow; use the synthetic monitoring story only when relevant to the buyer's question.

## 5. Separate selling gates from customer-software gates

Current source supports expanded intake, deterministic triage, human review/decision flows, versioned controls, audit records, and the new fictional-document evidence workflow. It still uses a shared passcode and selected persona. The evidence feature has no malware scanning, and the telemetry connector makes no live tracing-backend call. A named fictional approval is not authenticated customer approval.

### Gate A: before accepting the first services engagement

- Align the offer, proposal, and pilot page with the actual service and fictional demo boundary. The current `/pilot` promise of a customer's working console needs narrowing until Gate B passes.
- Prepare the sample pack, statement of work, acceptance criteria, payment/invoicing process, delivery calendar, and a named support contact.
- Have appropriate advisers review contract/data terms, IP ownership, reuse permission, and any specialist obligations. Document permitted data, access, retention, and customer approval of external responses.
- Assemble a concise trust brief: architecture/data flow, subprocessors used, current limitations, security contact, and truthful certification status. Make no certification claim without evidence.

### Gate B: before a customer operates Jeeves with real organizational data

Prefer a dedicated application deployment and database per initial customer, with a completely separate fictional demo. This can reduce early cross-customer exposure; it does not replace user authorization, operational controls, or testing.

| Workstream | Required proof before acceptance | Accountable owner |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and authority | Real IdP-backed users, server-assigned roles and domain assignments, stable user IDs, session revocation, and denial of unauthorized signatures/approvals | Engineering plus customer identity owner |
| Customer data boundary | Customer-specific configuration and access rules; tests for reads, writes, files, exports, jobs, and support access; no public access to customer records | Engineering/security |
| Evidence protection | Private storage, fail-closed malware quarantine, scoped downloads, retention/erasure design reconciled with audit-history requirements, and hosted upload/download checks | Engineering/security plus customer data owner |
| Reliability and recovery | Isolated staging, real Postgres migration/concurrency checks, witnessed backup restore, retry/restart recovery, audit-integrity checks, and a documented support/incident process | Engineering/operations |
| Policy and AI behavior | Customer-owned/versioned policy; reviewers validate routing and authority; provider data terms agreed; drafts tested on representative permitted examples or AI disabled | Customer governance owner plus engineering |
| Operating acceptance | One complete customer workflow passes acceptance; agreed availability/support scope; export and offboarding rehearsal; relevant security findings resolved | Customer sponsor plus founder |

Budget the engineering work only after discovery defines the identity provider, hosting needs, data classes, and acceptance criteria. Do not assume an SSO button alone completes this gate. SCIM, shared multi-tenancy, new connectors, and broad workflow customization wait unless the first contract requires them. Manual onboarding/offboarding must still be controlled and tested.

The repository currently mandates fictional data only. Gate B also requires an explicit human-approved production scope and amendment to that boundary. This plan does not change it. PHI and clinical/coverage decision use remain excluded from the first software pilot.

### Gate C: before broader production subscriptions

Prove the agreed workload, operational monitoring and incident response, restore objectives, permissions lifecycle, cost/usage controls, and sustained customer use. Add a real integration only for a contracted need, with evidence of source identity, freshness, failure behavior, and end-to-end operation. A paused state inside Jeeves must not be described as stopping an external AI system without a verified enforcement integration.

Do not make certifications, marketplace listings, more dashboards, or a full platform rewrite prerequisites to discovery. If a particular buyer requires certification before purchase, qualify that constraint early and cost it explicitly.

## 6. Measure value without turning hopes into claims

Establish a baseline during kickoff. For the vendor engagement, track source-backed requirements/total agreed requirements, hours assembling the response, customer correction rounds, unresolved gaps, actual reuse in the buyer response, and delivery cost. Missing evidence identified honestly is a valid result; a completed answer is not proof the underlying control works.

For a payer software pilot, use a separate scorecard: elapsed time from complete submission to human decision, active reviewer time, first-pass completeness, evidence retrieval time, and overdue follow-ups. Compare similar cases and record sample size. A small pilot is directional evidence, not proof of general savings.

Expansion requires the buyer to identify continuing value and pay for it. After two pilots, invest in automation only where the same steps repeat. If every engagement needs a different schema, policy interpretation, or bespoke report, decide explicitly whether this is a services business or whether the segment needs narrowing.
